Introduction

DoubleTake makes one performance sound like several. It creates two or four copies of your track — voices — and gives each its own small delay, a slightly different pitch, a slow pitch drift and its own place in the stereo field. Played together with the original, the result sounds like the part was sung or played more than once: thicker, wider and more alive.

Use it on lead and backing vocals, guitars, synth pads and leads, or anything recorded once that should sound bigger. With the octave settings and feedback it also goes further: octave doubles, slapback, chorus clouds and robotic stacks.

Quick start

  1. Insert DoubleTake on a vocal or instrument track. It loads the Natural Double preset: two voices, left and right.
  2. Play the track. Turn MIX towards 0% to hear less of the voices, towards 100% for voices only. 50% plays the original and the voices at full level.
  3. For a wider sound, click 4 VOICES, or pick another preset with the arrows in the title bar.
  4. Fine-tune a voice in the table: more DELAY sounds looser, more DETUNE sounds thicker, a little DEPTH keeps it moving.
Keep the main vocal mono-compatible: check the mix in mono now and then. Voices that are detuned and panned hard left and right can partly cancel in mono; bringing PAN in a little or lowering DETUNE fixes it.

Signal path

Input→Voices 1–4: delay → pitch → level, pan (with feedback)→Low / high shelf→Wet→+ Direct→Output

Every voice is fed the same mono sum of the input, so a stereo track becomes a set of voices placed exactly where you pan them. The direct signal keeps its original stereo image.

Displays

1Stage

A view from above: you are the triangle at the bottom. Left–right is each voice's PAN; the further back a dot sits, the longer its DELAY (0–100 ms). The white dot is the direct signal. Voices that are switched off, or not in use in 2-voice mode, fade out.

2Time & pitch

Each voice plotted by DELAY (across, 0–100 ms) and DETUNE (up and down, ±50 cents). The pale bar shows how far the wobble swings (DEPTH), and the dot moves along it at the voice's RATE. A voice shifted by an octave is tagged +8va or −8va.

3Wet EQ

The tone of the voices — not the direct signal. Drag L (low shelf) or H (high shelf): left–right sets the frequency, up–down the gain (±18 dB). Double-click the display to flatten both. The curve is the real response.

Cutting the lows of the voices (L down, around 150–250 Hz) keeps the double from making the track muddy; a gentle high lift (H up 2–3 dB) puts air on the copies so they sit behind the lead.

Global row

#ControlWhat it does
4Direct (button, GAIN, PAN)The original signal. The round button turns it on or off — off gives voices only. GAIN: −24 to +6 dB. PAN works as a balance: centre keeps both sides at full level.
5AlignThe pitch shifter inside each voice always lags by about 12 ms. With ALIGN ON (default) the direct signal is delayed by the same amount, so a voice's DELAY is its true distance from the original, and the plugin reports this latency to your DAW so the track stays in time. OFF gives zero latency; every voice then comes 12 ms later than its DELAY says.
6Feedback low-cutA low-cut filter (20 Hz – 1 kHz) inside every voice's feedback loop. Each repeat loses more low end, so long feedback stays clear instead of booming. It has no effect when FDBK is 0%.
7Modulation · Re-syncRestarts the wobble of every voice from the same point. Use it when automating, or to get a repeatable start for a bounce.
8Low shelf / High shelfThe same two shelves as the Wet EQ display, as a knob (gain ±18 dB) and a number box (frequency: low 20–500 Hz, high 1–16 kHz).
9Mix0% = direct only · 50% (typical) = direct and voices both at full level · 100% = voices only. Use it as one knob for “how much double”.

Drag a number box or knob up or down to change it; hold Shift for fine steps; double-click to reset. In 2-voice mode, voices 3 and 4 are dimmed and silent.

11Output

Two console faders. WET sets the level of all voices together (after the shelves), OUT the level of everything. Both run from +10 dB to −∞ on a console scale, with fine steps around 0 dB. The stereo meter shows the output peak, −60 to 0 dBFS.

Recipes

  • Backing vocals that sound like a group: 4 voices, DELAY 15 / 25 / 35 / 45 ms, DETUNE ±8 and ±15, DEPTH 6–10, PAN spread wide, MIX 60–70%.
  • Wider guitar without a second take: 2 voices hard left and right, DELAY 14 and 22 ms, DETUNE ±6, DEPTH 3. Pan the original guitar centre or turn DIRECT off for a pure stereo pair.
  • Synth lead octave thickening: voice 1 OCT +1 at −14 dB, voice 2 OCT −1 at −10 dB, both centred, DELAY 0–5 ms.
  • Dub-style echo: one voice, DELAY 100 ms, FDBK 60–70%, feedback low-cut 300 Hz, high shelf −6 dB.

Technical notes

  • Formats: AU and VST3, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel). Mono or stereo in, stereo out.
  • Pitch shifting: a two-head rotating-tap shifter with a 24 ms window and cubic interpolation; octave and detune are exact. Like any short-window shifter it adds a slight grain to sustained pure tones when shifting by an octave — on voices and instruments this reads as part of the double.
  • Latency: about 12 ms (577 samples at 48 kHz) with ALIGN on, reported to the host for automatic compensation; 0 with ALIGN off.
  • Feedback: up to 90%, through a one-pole low-cut, always stable.
  • Shelves: second-order (RBJ) low and high shelf on the voices.
  • Tail: 3 seconds reported, so the repeats are not cut off when playback stops.
