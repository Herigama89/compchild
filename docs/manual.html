Introduction

Compchild is a two-channel vari-mu (variable-mu) tube limiter in the spirit of the classic 1950s broadcast limiters. A vari-mu compressor turns its gain down by changing the bias of its tubes, which gives a very smooth, program-dependent kind of compression: it starts gently, gets firmer the harder you push, and releases in a natural, musical way. It is equally at home gluing a mix bus, levelling a vocal or adding weight to drums.

Each of the two channels (A and B) has its own Threshold, Input Gain, Time Constant and DC Bias. The channels can work as left / right, as lateral / vertical (sum and difference) or as mid / side, and can be linked so one set of knobs controls both.

Quick start

  1. Insert Compchild on a mixer track or bus. It loads with Link on, Threshold 5 and Time Constant 3 — a good general setting.
  2. Play your audio and watch the GR meters. Turn Threshold up until the needles move left by the amount of compression you want (2–4 dB for gentle glue, 6–10 dB for obvious levelling).
  3. Choose a Time Constant: lower numbers are faster and punchier, higher numbers slower and smoother.
  4. Use Output to match the level with Compchild switched off (click the Power lamp to compare).
Like the tube units it is modelled on, Compchild has a fixed gain of about +8.5 dB at its default settings, so signals come out louder even before they are compressed. Turn Output down to level-match.

Parameters

1Threshold

Sets how much Compchild compresses. 0 means no compression at all; turning it up lowers the level at which compression begins, so more of the signal is turned down. Range 0–10, default 5.

KnobCompression starts at (input level)
0off — clean, no compression
3about −26.6 dBFS
5 (default)about −31.5 dBFS
8about −35.3 dBFS
10about −37.4 dBFS

Above the threshold the ratio is not fixed: it starts very gently (about 1.6 : 1) and rises steadily the further the signal goes over (about 3 : 1, then 7 : 1, and up to about 13 : 1 at the top), ending up as a limiter. That rising ratio is the vari-mu sound.

The threshold is measured after Input Gain, so turning Input Gain up also makes Compchild compress more.

2Input Gain

Sets the level going into the tube stage, from −10 dB to +10 dB (default 0 dB). Turning it up pushes the signal harder into the compressor: you get more compression and more of the low-frequency “grip” that vari-mu limiters are known for. Turning it down gives a lighter touch.

Below the threshold the path is clean; the extra character comes from the compression itself (see Technical notes).

3Time Constant

Chooses how fast Compchild reacts to loud sounds (attack) and how quickly it lets go afterwards (release). Six fixed positions, like the original hardware; default 3.

TCAttackReleaseCharacter
1~1 msvery fast (~0.04 s)grabs and lets go quickly — aggressive, can pump
2~1 msfast (~0.12 s)punchy — drums, vocals
3~1.5 msmedium (~0.36 s)smooth all-rounder (default)
4~3 msslow (~0.7 s)gentle — bus and mix glue
5~1.5 msmedium + long tail (~2.5 s)program-dependent: quick on short peaks, holds on sustained loud passages
6~1 msfast + very long tail (~4.5 s)most program-dependent — mastering, steady levelling

Release times are the time to recover about 63 % of the gain reduction. Compchild releases in two stages, like the real thing: roughly 60 % of the compression recovers at the speed shown and the rest follows about four times slower. That slow tail is a big part of the smooth vari-mu feel.

4DC Bias

Sets the knee — how gradually compression eases in around the threshold. It does not change the overall amount of compression, only how it starts. Range − to +, default centre.

  • Centre (0) — an 11 dB soft knee: the classic, balanced character.
  • Towards − — harder knee (down to about 5.5 dB): grabs more abruptly once over the threshold. Punchier and more obvious, closer to a limiter.
  • Towards + — softer knee (up to about 22 dB): compression starts earlier but very lightly and eases in over a wide range. Smoother and more transparent — great for mix-bus glue.

The name comes from the bias adjustment on vintage vari-mu units, where changing the tubes' DC bias voltage changed how gradually they began to compress.

5GR meters

The two meters show the gain reduction of channel A (left meter) and channel B (right meter). The needle rests on 0 when nothing is being compressed and swings left as Compchild turns the signal down: pointing at 3 means 3 dB of gain reduction, 10 means 10 dB, and so on.

The red section (1–3) and the small 40–100 scale are traditional VU markings. When Power is off the needles drop to the left stop.

Click either meter to switch between the cream and black meter skins.

6Output

Final level after compression, from −10 dB to +10 dB (default 0 dB). Use it to make up for the level lost to compression, or to match the level with the plugin bypassed.

The output never goes above −0.04 dBFS: anything louder is clipped at that ceiling, just as the original unit does. If you hear harsh distortion on peaks, turn Output down a little.

7Mode

Chooses what the two channels process. Click and drag the switch (or use the mouse wheel).

  • L / R — channel A processes the left side, channel B the right side.
  • LAT / VERT (lateral / vertical) — the classic sum-and-difference mode of the original broadcast limiters. Channel A compresses the sum (what is common to both sides, the centre), channel B the difference (the stereo width). Useful to control a loud centre without squashing the sides, or the other way round.
  • MID / SIDE — channel A compresses the mid; the side signal passes through uncompressed (channel B's controls have no effect in this mode). Tightens the centre of a mix while keeping the stereo width open.

8Link

Click the lamp (or the word LINK) to switch it. When lit (red), channel A's knobs control both channels — moving a channel A knob moves the matching channel B knob with it — and in L / R mode both channels share the same gain reduction, which keeps the stereo image steady. Turning Link on copies channel A's settings to channel B.

Switch Link off to set the two channels independently. Default: on.

9Power

Click the lamp (or the word POWER) to switch Compchild on or off. When lit, the plugin is working; when dark, the audio passes through untouched (bypass) and the meter needles drop. Switching fades smoothly over a few milliseconds, so there are no clicks — handy for quick before/after comparisons.

10Manual ( ? )

Click the small ? beside CHANNEL B to open this manual in your web browser. It is also in the right-click menu (Open manual…).

Technical notes

  • Formats: AU and VST3, macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel). Mono and stereo.
  • Latency: zero samples. Works at any sample rate.
  • Sound: clean and flat (20 Hz–20 kHz) below the threshold. When compressing, the gain rides slightly within each low-frequency cycle, which adds a little warmth to the low end that grows as you compress harder — about 1.3 % distortion at 100 Hz under full limiting, much less in the mids.
  • Output ceiling: −0.04 dBFS.
  • CPU: very light; use as many instances as you like.
